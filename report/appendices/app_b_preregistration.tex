\chapter{Pre-registration and Provenance}
\label{app:prereg}

This appendix records the provenance of the real-data experiment: what was fixed in advance, the
hashes that prove when, the commit chain, and the one protocol field that was later revised.

\section{The R1 pre-registration}

\subsection*{What the document fixes}

The pre-registration settles every choice that could otherwise have been made after seeing a result.
Table~\ref{tab:prereg} in Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} summarises the substance and is not repeated
here. In outline the document fixes: the ground-truth definition (the dense segmentation support,
with centroid, box-centre, brightest-cell and sparse-centroid alternatives explicitly rejected); the
eligible region and the definition of an eligible object; the matching rule and its tie-breaking
order; the primary metric and the secondary tolerance ladder; the strata to be reported; the
statistical plan, including the sequence-balanced bootstrap; the threshold rule for the learned arm;
which CFAR variant is primary; and the wording permitted when describing unmatched detections.

Two rulings were applied at the final freeze and are recorded because they changed the protocol
after the draft:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item The learned detector's global threshold rule was approved as proposed --- derived per seed,
from synthetic validation data only, as the lowest threshold whose pooled measured per-cell
$\Pfa$ does not exceed the target.
\item The primary CFAR comparator was changed from OS to CA.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Adapter equivalence checks}

Two numerical checks confirm that the frozen detectors receive real data without any preprocessing
being redesigned, as Section~\ref{sec:r0} states.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{CFAR scale invariance.} Scaling the input by factors from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{6}$ left
the CFAR statistic unchanged to $1.5 \times 10^{-11}$ and produced identical peak sets, confirming
that the unknown constant relating the published array to absolute power cancels in the ratio test.
\item \textbf{Network preprocessing.} Feeding $10^{\,D/20}$ to the unmodified frozen preprocessing
code reproduces the analytic reduction of Equation~\ref{eq:cnn_input} to $2.4 \times 10^{-7}$,
about two units in the last place of the single-precision output.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Dataset reduction check}

The CARRADA distribution stores every annotation twice: as dense per-frame arrays and as a compact
coordinate file. Before the redundant arrays were deleted, masks reconstructed from the coordinate
file were compared against the stored arrays on \textbf{360 sampled frame--annotation-type pairs}
spanning all \CarradaSequences{} sequences. Every pair was byte-identical, so the reduction
described in Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} discards no annotation content.

\subsection*{Isolation manifest}

Before any real data was processed, a manifest recorded the SHA-256 hash, size and protection
category of \textbf{628 files}: the eight frozen network checkpoints and their training
configurations; the network, CFAR, evaluation and radar source modules; the frozen configuration
file; the calibrated threshold table; the dataset configurations and caches; the headline result
tables; the CFAR baseline outputs; and the complete outputs of the three attribution experiments.
The manifest was re-verified at the end of every phase, and no protected file changed at any point.

Every test-split access event is recorded with its timestamp, the repository commit, the
pre-registration hash, the run-manifest hash, the environment, and the hash of every model
checkpoint used. Raw detector output was written to disk and hashed before any summary statistic was
computed, so every table and figure in Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} is traceable to immutable
evidence.

\section{Revision of the post-processing protocol}
\label{app:nmsrevision}

The results reported in Chapters~\ref{chp:synthetic} and~\ref{chp:realdata} were produced at a
non-maximum-suppression radius of \ProtocolNms{}. An earlier version of this work used radius
\ProtocolNmsOriginal{}. That change is recorded here in full, because the real-data comparison of
Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} is sensitive to it and the reader is entitled to know when the choice was
made.

\subsection*{What happened}

The frozen configuration fixed the suppression radius at \ProtocolNmsOriginal{}
($\ProtocolNmsOriginalWin \times \ProtocolNmsOriginalWin$ cells) before the synthetic benchmark was
scored, on the basis of a development observation that Appendix~\ref{app:corrections} now records as
Correction~C9. When that justification was checked against a controlled sweep it did not hold. The
sweep was therefore run properly, on development data only, under a selection rule written and
hash-frozen \emph{before} any measurement was taken, and it selected radius \ProtocolNms{}: the
smallest radius tested that produced no duplicate in-gate detections.

\subsection*{What was re-measured, and on what data}

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The radius was selected on synthetic development data alone} --- the tuning split for
selection, the validation split for confirmation. The final synthetic holdout was not used, and
\textbf{no real data of any kind entered the choice}.
\item \textbf{The synthetic benchmark was re-scored on a freshly generated holdout} of
\SynHoldoutFrames{} frames, produced from a new seed after the radius was fixed, and verified to
share no frame content with any previously generated split. The superseded test split was not
re-used, so the revised benchmark is a genuine one-shot read rather than a second look at data whose
answer was already known.
\item \textbf{The real-data evaluation was re-run} under the revised protocol, on the same official
CARRADA test sequences, with thresholds re-derived from synthetic validation data alone.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What did not change}

The detector itself is untouched. The same eight checkpoints at epoch \TinyEpochs{} were loaded,
with the same weights; nothing was retrained, fine-tuned, re-initialised or re-selected. The CFAR
window geometry, the threshold multipliers, the input transform, the label definition, the matching
rule, the operating-point definition, the tolerance ladder and the statistical treatment are all as
specified in Chapter~\ref{chp:detectors}. \textbf{One post-processing constant changed, and nothing
else.}

\subsection*{What the reader should not conclude}

The real-data evaluation reported in Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} remains zero-shot in the sense that
matters: no real measurement was used for training, fine-tuning, threshold calibration or radius
selection. It is \textbf{not}, however, the original untouched pre-registered run. It is a re-run of
the pre-registered protocol with one post-processing field revised, and it was performed after the
original result was known. The original radius-\ProtocolNmsOriginal{} outputs have not been deleted,
overwritten or edited; they remain on disk beside the revised ones, along with the frozen selection
rule and its hash.

\subsection*{The attribution experiments were re-scored, not re-run}
\label{app:attribrescore}

The three attribution experiments of Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} were designed, run and first
analysed under the original radius, before the revision described above. Rather than report them
under a protocol the rest of the thesis no longer uses, they were re-scored at radius
\ProtocolNms{} before submission, and Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} quotes only those re-scored
values. What that involved, precisely:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{No model was retrained.} Every checkpoint was loaded from its original experiment
folder with its original weights. Nothing was retrained, fine-tuned, re-initialised or re-selected.
E2 is a classical detector and has no weights at all.
\item \textbf{Inference was regenerated only where it had to be.} Candidate extraction discards the
score map once the local maxima are taken, and the maxima of a wider window are a strict subset of
those of a narrower one, so a smaller radius cannot be recovered from a stored candidate list.
E3's earlier checkpoints and both E4 arms were therefore passed through their frozen networks
again. E2's integrated detections were re-extracted from the range--Doppler data.
\item \textbf{Baselines were re-scored to match.} Each experiment compares against cached
plain-CFAR and TinyCNN candidates. Those caches were rebuilt at radius \ProtocolNms{}; comparing
revised detections against original-radius baselines would have been a mixed measurement.
\item \textbf{Nothing else changed.} Split, seeds, epoch, operating-point definition, matching rule,
tolerance gates, bootstrap seed and Bonferroni correction are all as originally registered. One
post-processing constant changed, and nothing else.
\item \textbf{No real data entered any of this.} All three experiments run on the synthetic
validation split. No CARRADA data of any split was opened.
\item \textbf{The frozen benchmark was not touched.} The held-out split of
Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic} is not read by any attribution experiment, at either radius.
\end{itemize}

The original outputs have not been deleted or overwritten. They remain, with their original values,
in the three experiment folders:

E2 & \texttt{experiments/integrated\_cfar/} \\
E3 & \texttt{experiments/label\_ablation/} \\
E4 & \texttt{experiments/e4\_cut\_guard\_mask/} \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

readings of every experiment remain on disk and comparable.

\subsection*{Why the change matters more on real data than on synthetic data}

The synthetic conclusions are almost unaffected by the radius, and the reason is geometric. A
simulated target is a point scatterer whose response spans about two bins, so any reasonable
suppression window contains exactly one maximum per target and shrinking it changes little. A
CARRADA road user is an extended region whose annotated support has a median extent of roughly
$18 \times 13$ bins --- \emph{larger than the suppression window itself}. There the window operates
inside a single object, and which local maximum survives is close to arbitrary. That is why the
real-data comparison moved and the synthetic one did not, and it is reported in
Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} as a finding in its own right rather than as an inconvenience.
