\chapter{Protocol Revisions and Sensitivity Checks}
\label{app:prereg}

Every substantive choice in this project --- ground truth, eligibility, matching, metrics, the
statistical plan, the threshold rule and the primary classical comparator --- was written down and
frozen before the corresponding evaluation data were scored. Two things changed afterwards, and both
affect how the final evidence should be read. They are recorded here in full. Everything else that
supports them --- freeze records with their hashes, the file manifests that protect completed
experiments from later modification, the commit chain, environment fingerprints and the complete
corrections log kept during development --- is retained in the accompanying project repository, along
with the raw detector outputs from which every table and figure in this report is derived.

\section{Revision of the post-processing protocol}
\label{app:nmsrevision}

\subsection*{What happened}

The frozen configuration fixed the non-maximum-suppression radius at \ProtocolNmsOriginal{}
($\ProtocolNmsOriginalWin \times \ProtocolNmsOriginalWin$ cells) before the synthetic benchmark was
scored, on the basis of a development observation. When that justification was checked against a
controlled sweep it did not hold. The sweep was therefore run properly, on development data only,
under a selection rule written and frozen \emph{before} any measurement was taken, and it selected
radius \ProtocolNms{}: the smallest radius tested that produced no duplicate in-gate detections.
\textbf{Every result in this report is at radius \ProtocolNms{}.}

\subsection*{What was re-measured, and on what data}

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The radius was selected on synthetic development data alone} --- the tuning split for
selection, the validation split for confirmation. The final synthetic holdout was not used, and
\textbf{no real data of any kind entered the choice}.
\item \textbf{The synthetic benchmark was re-scored on a freshly generated holdout} of
\SynHoldoutFrames{} frames, produced from a new seed after the radius was fixed, and verified to
share no frame content with any previously generated split. The superseded test split was not
re-used, so the revised benchmark is a genuine one-shot read rather than a second look at data whose
answer was already known. This is why Chapters~\ref{chp:simulator} and~\ref{chp:synthetic} describe a
new holdout rather than a re-scored one.
\item \textbf{The real-data evaluation was re-run} under the revised protocol, on the same official
CARRADA test sequences, with thresholds re-derived from synthetic validation data alone.
\item \textbf{The three attribution experiments were re-scored} at radius \ProtocolNms{} from their
original checkpoints. No model was retrained, fine-tuned, re-initialised or re-selected; because a
wider window's local maxima are a strict subset of a narrower one's, a smaller radius cannot be
recovered from a stored candidate list, so inference was regenerated and the cached CFAR and TinyCNN
baselines were rebuilt at the same radius. Split, seeds, epoch, operating-point definition, matching
rule, tolerance gates, bootstrap seed and Bonferroni correction are unchanged. No CARRADA data of any
split was opened, and the held-out synthetic split was not read.
\label{app:attribrescore}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{What did not change, and what the reader should conclude}

The detectors are untouched. The same eight checkpoints at epoch \TinyEpochs{} were loaded with the
same weights. The CFAR window geometry, the threshold multipliers, the input transform, the label
definition, the matching rule, the operating-point definition, the tolerance ladder and the
statistical treatment are all as specified in Chapter~\ref{chp:detectors}. \textbf{One
post-processing constant changed, and nothing else.}

The real-data evaluation of Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} therefore remains zero-shot in the sense that
matters: no real measurement was used for training, fine-tuning, threshold calibration or radius
selection. It is \textbf{not}, however, the original untouched run. It is a re-run of the frozen
protocol with one post-processing field revised, performed after the original result was known. The
original radius-\ProtocolNmsOriginal{} outputs remain on disk beside the revised ones, together with
the frozen selection rule.

\subsection*{Why the change matters more on real data than on synthetic data}

The synthetic conclusions are almost unaffected by the radius, and the reason is geometric. A
simulated target is a point scatterer whose response spans about two bins, so any reasonable
suppression window contains exactly one maximum per target and shrinking it changes little. A CARRADA
road user is an extended region whose annotated support has a median extent of roughly
$18 \times 13$ bins --- \emph{larger than the suppression window itself}. There the window operates
inside a single object, and which local maximum survives is close to arbitrary. That is why the
real-data comparison moved and the synthetic one did not, and Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} reports it
as a finding in its own right rather than as an inconvenience.

\section{A defect in the threshold-derivation code}
\label{app:thrdefect}

\subsection*{What happened}

\texttt{predict\_points} declares its suppression radius as a default argument, and a Python default
binds once, when the function is defined --- not when it is called. The module-level constant it
binds to is the original radius. The routine that collects a whole split calls
\texttt{predict\_points} without passing a radius, so it always extracted candidates at the
\emph{original} radius regardless of what a caller set afterwards. The revised-radius copy sets the
constant on a different module, which reaches the per-frame helpers but not that collector.

\textbf{Consequence.} The zero-shot thresholds published as radius-\ProtocolNms{} were in fact
derived at radius \ProtocolNmsOriginal{}, which is why they are byte-identical to the
radius-\ProtocolNmsOriginal{} set. Candidate extraction during the evaluation itself was unaffected,
because that path passes the radius explicitly. The reported evaluation therefore ran at radius
\ProtocolNms{} with thresholds derived at radius \ProtocolNmsOriginal{}. Because the smaller radius
keeps a superset of candidates, the rule-correct threshold would have been slightly higher, and the
learned arm operated slightly above its intended output level.

\subsection*{Sensitivity analysis}

The size of the effect was measured rather than argued. The domain intervention of
Section~\ref{sec:stage4} derived its thresholds \emph{both} ways and ran the comparison twice. The
primary difference was \CiTestDelta{} under the original code path and \CiTestDeltaRtwo{} under the
corrected one --- the same conclusion, the same sign, the same significance.

\textbf{Conclusion: the defect is real and the interpretation is materially unchanged.} It is
recorded because the published thresholds were not derived under the rule they were described by,
not because it changes any result in this report.

\section{Chronology of the follow-up experiments}

Chapters~\ref{chp:synthetic} to~\ref{chp:realdata} report the work as originally planned: the
synthetic benchmark, the attribution experiments, and one evaluation of the CARRADA test split under
a protocol frozen beforehand. The two experiments of Chapter~\ref{chp:domaingap} came afterwards, in
response to that result, and are reported as follow-up work rather than folded back into it.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Stage~4} (Section~\ref{sec:stage4}) calibrated a revised synthetic background from the
18 official CARRADA \emph{training} sequences and evaluated the resulting networks on the CARRADA
test split --- a split whose original zero-shot result was already known, which is why Stage~4 is
described as a domain intervention and not as a fresh external validation. No real frame entered
neural optimisation or threshold calibration.
\item \textbf{Stage~5} (Section~\ref{sec:stage5}) fine-tuned on CARRADA training sequences, fixed its
operating point on three held-out training sequences, and then read the official CARRADA
\emph{validation} split once. Those sequences had never been scored by any detector in this project,
which is why they are described as \emph{previously unscored} rather than unseen: the domain audit of
Section~\ref{sec:r7audit} read their frames to measure background statistics, with no detector
involved and no label used.
\item \textbf{The CARRADA test split was not opened during Stage~5}, and the validation split was not
opened during Stages~1--4.
\end{itemize}
