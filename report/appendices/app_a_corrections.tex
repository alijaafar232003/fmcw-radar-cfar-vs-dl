\section{C9 --- The stated justification for the suppression radius}

\textbf{What was claimed.} An earlier draft of Section~\ref{sec:nmschoice} stated that a
suppression radius of \ProtocolNmsOriginal{} had been selected because it ``performed substantially
better on isolated targets'' than smaller radii, and that a smaller radius costs detection
probability across every condition by admitting more candidates and forcing a higher threshold at
fixed measured $\Pfa$.

\textbf{Why it was wrong.} The claim was never measured in the form in which it was stated. A
controlled sweep over radii $1$, $2$, $3$ and $5$, run on the tuning split and confirmed on the
validation split, finds isolated-target $\Pd$ flat to within $0.005$ across the entire range, for
the network and for every CFAR variant. The mechanism the claim rested on --- candidate density
rising sharply as the radius falls --- is real and large, and is reported in
Figure~\ref{fig:nmssel}; the detection consequence attributed to it is not there once the operating
point is rematched. The original observation appears to have come from a development sweep that
watched a single arm, at a stage of the work before the benchmark data existed.

\textbf{What replaced it.} Section~\ref{sec:nmschoice} now reports the full sweep and states the
criterion that the measurements actually support: radius \ProtocolNms{} is the smallest tested
radius that avoids measurable duplicate inflation while retaining close-target separation. The
protocol consequence of that correction is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}, since it changed
the reported benchmark rather than only the text describing it.

\textbf{What did not change.} No model was retrained or fine-tuned, no checkpoint was altered, and
no threshold was re-derived from anything other than synthetic development data.
