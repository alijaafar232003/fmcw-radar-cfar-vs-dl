\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) detection is the standard approach to target detection in radar,
and a growing literature reports that learned detectors outperform it. Those comparisons are hard to
interpret, because the two arms often differ in their inputs, in the context they observe, or in
whether false-alarm rates are matched by design or by measurement.

A \TinyParams{}-parameter convolutional detector, whose receptive field is matched to the CFAR
reference window, is compared against the CA, GO, SO and OS variants of CFAR on simulated FMCW
range--Doppler maps. Both arms are scored by a single evaluator at a matched \emph{measured}
false-alarm rate, across \SynConditions{} conditions spanning regimes where CFAR is
near-optimal and regimes where it is documented to fail. Every network value is a mean over
\TinySeeds{} seeds.

On a held-out split read once, the network establishes a gain on \SynWins{} of the
\SynConditions{} conditions after correction for multiple comparisons; the unresolved stratum shows
a small difference (\SynUnresolvedDiff) that does not survive correction, and homogeneous clutter is
a practical tie (\SynClutterDiff). In decibels, the network reaches half detection at a cell
signal-to-noise ratio \SI{\SynSnrGainDL}{\deci\bel} below CA-CFAR. That advantage is paid for in
placement: pooled localisation error is \LocRmseRatio{} times CA-CFAR's, and \LocDvRatio{} times
worse on the Doppler axis.

Three pre-registered attribution experiments narrow the mechanism without isolating it. Giving CFAR
the same integrated mainlobe energy makes it worse, so energy integration does not explain the gain.
Flat $3\times3$ supervision contributes strongly to the localisation deficit, and a weighted label
roughly halves that error at no detection cost. Denying the network its guard cells costs little
detection and sharpens its response.

Evaluated zero-shot on the CARRADA automotive radar dataset, with no retraining or recalibration,
the two detectors are statistically indistinguishable at exact annotated support (paired difference
\RoneDelta{}, \SI{95}{\percent} interval $[\RoneCIlo, \RoneCIhi]$), while the network emits
\RoneBurdenRatio{} times as many unmatched detections per frame. The synthetic advantage does not
transfer cleanly to real measurements, and detection probability alone cannot rank the two
detectors.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} FMCW radar, range--Doppler processing, CFAR, convolutional neural
networks, domain shift.
