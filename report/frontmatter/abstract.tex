\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\sloppy
Constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) is a standard method for radar target detection, while
deep-learning detectors are increasingly reported to outperform it. However, these comparisons are
often difficult to interpret because the methods may use different inputs, different spatial
context, or different operating points. More importantly, a gain measured in simulation may not
survive on real radar data. This thesis therefore asks a broader question: under what conditions can
a learned detector outperform CFAR?

A small \TinyParams{}-parameter convolutional network, TinyCNN, is compared with four CFAR variants
on FMCW range--Doppler maps. The detectors use comparable local context and are evaluated through a
common pipeline. The study then follows the learned detector through five stages: controlled
synthetic comparison, attribution experiments, zero-shot transfer to real CARRADA radar data,
domain-informed synthetic training, and real-data fine-tuning.

On synthetic data, TinyCNN significantly outperforms CFAR in five challenging conditions, while
homogeneous clutter is effectively a tie. The attribution experiments show that this gain is not
simply caused by integrating more target energy, and that the network's localisation behaviour
depends strongly on how it is trained.

The advantage does not transfer automatically. On CARRADA, the original synthetic-trained TinyCNN
and CA-CFAR achieve statistically comparable recall, while TinyCNN produces almost twice as many
unmatched detections. Making the synthetic training data more similar to CARRADA substantially
improves recall, but also produces much denser output.

Finally, after fine-tuning on real CARRADA training data, TinyCNN reaches \FtValFt{} recall compared
with \FtValCFAR{} for CA-CFAR, while producing slightly fewer unmatched detections per frame
(\FtValBurdenFt{} versus \FtValBurdenCFAR{}). The improvement over the previous learned model is
mainly in selectivity rather than recall.

The results therefore support a conditional conclusion: CFAR is the stronger training-free baseline,
while the learned detector achieves a higher performance ceiling when its training data represent
the deployment domain.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} FMCW radar, CFAR, convolutional neural networks, range--Doppler maps,
domain adaptation.
