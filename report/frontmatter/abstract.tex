\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) detection remains a standard approach to radar target detection,
while recent work increasingly reports improvements from learned detectors. Such comparisons can be
difficult to interpret when the methods observe different inputs, use different spatial context, or
are evaluated at unmatched false-alarm rates. This work compares a lightweight
\TinyParams{}-parameter convolutional detector (TinyCNN) with CA-, GO-, SO- and OS-CFAR on simulated
FMCW range--Doppler maps using comparable $\TinyRFR \times \TinyRFD$ local context and a shared
downstream evaluation pipeline. Performance is compared at matched measured false-alarm rate across
conditions covering isolated targets, multiple targets, masking, clutter edges, mixed scenes and
homogeneous clutter, with an additional unresolved-target condition used as a resolution and
protocol stress test.

On a fresh held-out synthetic test set, the network establishes significant gains across the five
resolvable challenge conditions, while homogeneous clutter is a practical tie. The unresolved stress
test remains near its expected structural limit and shows no significant difference after
correction. The network reaches $\Pd = 0.5$ at a signal-to-noise ratio
\SI{\SynSnrGainDL}{\deci\bel} below CA-CFAR.

The frozen benchmark network initially shows poorer localisation, but attribution experiments
demonstrate that much of this deficit is induced by the flat $3\times3$ supervision rather than
being inherent to the learned detector; weighted supervision substantially improves localisation
with negligible detection cost. Further experiments show that simple mainlobe-energy integration
does not explain the learned advantage, while access to guard-region information modestly influences
the learned response.

Zero-shot evaluation on CARRADA shows no statistically significant difference between TinyCNN and
CA-CFAR at exact annotated support, while the network produces approximately
\RoneBurdenRatio{}$\times$ more unmatched detections per frame. These results show that a
lightweight learned detector can outperform CFAR under controlled synthetic conditions, but that
this advantage does not transfer cleanly to real radar measurements and must be considered together
with localisation and output-density trade-offs.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} FMCW radar, range--Doppler processing, CFAR, convolutional neural
networks, domain shift.
