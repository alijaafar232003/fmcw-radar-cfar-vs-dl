\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\begin{spacing}{1.12}
Constant false-alarm rate (CFAR) detection remains the radar standard, while learned detectors
increasingly report improvements over it. Such comparisons are hard to interpret when the methods
observe different inputs, use different spatial context or are scored at unmatched false-alarm rates,
and rarely test whether a simulated gain survives on real data. This thesis therefore asks not
whether a learned detector beats CFAR, but \emph{under what conditions} it does.

A \TinyParams{}-parameter convolutional detector (TinyCNN) is compared with CA-, GO-, SO- and
OS-CFAR on simulated FMCW range--Doppler maps, using comparable $\TinyRFR \times \TinyRFD$ local
context, one shared evaluation pipeline and a matched measured false-alarm rate. It is then carried
through four further stages: attribution, zero-shot transfer to the CARRADA automotive radar dataset,
target-domain-informed synthetic training, and supervised real-domain fine-tuning.

On a held-out synthetic test set the network establishes significant gains in five challenge
conditions, ties in homogeneous clutter, and shows no significant difference on an unresolved-target
stress case. Attribution shows the advantage is not simple mainlobe-energy integration, and that its
localisation behaviour is largely set by how it is supervised.

Transfer changes the picture. Zero-shot on the CARRADA test split the two are statistically
indistinguishable in exact dense-mask recall, while the network emits roughly \RoneBurdenRatio{}
times more unmatched detections per frame. Rebuilding the synthetic training background from CARRADA
\emph{training}-split statistics alone, with no real frame entering neural optimisation, raised exact
test recall from \CiTestOrig{} to \CiTestNew{} --- but at \CiTestBurdenRatio{} times the output
density, with no superiority over CA-CFAR under burden matching.

Finally, fine-tuning on CARRADA training sequences, with the operating point fixed on training data
alone, gave exact recall \FtValFt{} against CA-CFAR's \FtValCFAR{} on previously unscored validation
sequences: \FtDelta{} (95\,\% CI [\FtCIlo{}, \FtCIhi{}]), at a slightly \emph{lower} burden of
\FtValBurdenFt{} against \FtValBurdenCFAR{} unmatched detections per frame. Against the
synthetic-only arm it established no recall gain; what it improved was the recall--output trade-off.

CFAR is therefore the stronger training-free detector: interpretable, cheap and transferable without
adaptation. The learned detector demonstrated the higher observed performance ceiling, but reached it
only when the deployment domain was represented during training.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} FMCW radar, CFAR, convolutional networks, domain adaptation.
\end{spacing}
