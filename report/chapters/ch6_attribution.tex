\ulfgchapter{6}{Attribution: Where Does the Learned Advantage Come From?}{chp:attribution}

\section{Introduction}

Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic} established that the learned detector holds a measured advantage on the
synthetic benchmark, and that the frozen arm places its detections less precisely than CFAR. Neither
result says \emph{why}. A margin reported
as a single unexplained number cannot be transferred, defended or designed against: without a
mechanism there is no way to predict whether the same advantage will appear under different
conditions, which is exactly the question Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} goes on to ask.

This chapter therefore states the candidate explanations and pairs each with an experiment capable
of disproving it. Three were run. Each was pre-registered in its own directory before any result
existed, each is isolated from the frozen artefacts, and none of them touched the held-out split.

\textbf{A note on scope that applies to the whole chapter.} All three attribution experiments are
reported under the final radius-\ProtocolNms{} post-processing protocol of
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}. They use the validation split only, and they do not alter the frozen
benchmark of Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic}. Section~\ref{sec:attrib_scope} states what that does and
does not permit.

\section{Motivation}

Three explanations for a learned advantage appear in the literature reviewed in
Section~\ref{sec:sota}, and they are not equivalent.

\begin{description}
\itemsep3pt
\item[Energy integration.] A single-cell CFAR test discards the target energy that spills into
neighbouring cells. A network with a $\TinyRFR \times \TinyRFD$ receptive field can combine it. If
this is the mechanism, giving CFAR the same integrated energy should close most of the gap.
\item[Supervision.] The $3\times3$ block label tells the network that nine cells are equally
correct. This is the documented suspect behind the localisation deficit of
Section~\ref{sec:localization}, and it predicts a response broader than one cell.
\item[Information access.] CFAR must discard its guard cells to estimate a background; the network
is under no such constraint and may use them. If that access is what matters, denying it should cost
detection.
\end{description}

The first is the cheap explanation and is tested first. The second and third are tested by
intervention rather than by argument.

\section{Integrated CFAR: is the advantage mainlobe energy?}

\textbf{What was tested.} CFAR's noise estimate was left exactly as it is --- the estimators are
imported from the project's own code and called unchanged --- and only the cell under test was
replaced by an integrated statistic over a $3\times3$ region. Two kernels were used: a uniform sum,
and a kernel weighted by the measured point spread function, which is the matched filter for this
response. The region was derived rather than assumed: a $1\times1$ cell captures \EtwoCapOne{} of a
target's energy, $3\times3$ captures \EtwoCapThree{} and $5\times5$ captures \EtwoCapFive{}, so
$3\times3$ takes almost all the available energy while still sitting strictly inside the guard
region, where it cannot contaminate the reference ring.

\textbf{The measured result.} Integration does not close the gap. It makes the classical detector
\emph{worse}, on every condition and for both kernels.

The ordering is informative. Performance is monotone in how sharply the kernel concentrates on the
centre: single-cell CFAR is best, the matched filter second, the uniform sum worst. Localisation
follows the same order --- averaged over the seven conditions, Doppler error rises to
\EtwoDvRatioPsf{} times CFAR's under the PSF kernel and \EtwoDvRatioUniform{} times under the uniform
one.

\textbf{What it supports.} The response is concentrated in very few cells, so summing nine of them
adds little signal and a great deal of noise; because the window functions correlate adjacent cells,
the noise sum grows faster than the square root of the cell count and the deflection falls. The
learned advantage is therefore \textbf{not} access to mainlobe energy that CFAR discards. The cheap
explanation is eliminated, which promotes the learned decision rule itself to the live one.

\textbf{Caveat.} This shows that \emph{this} form of integration, with the noise estimate held
fixed, does not reproduce the advantage. It does not show that the network gains nothing from its
receptive field: a network can weight that field in ways a fixed $3\times3$ kernel cannot, including
adaptively.

\section{Label ablation: what causes the localisation deficit?}

\textbf{What was tested.} Three label representations, eight seeds each, trained identically and
otherwise matching the frozen configuration: a single-cell \emph{point} label, the incumbent
\emph{flat} $3\times3$ block, and a \emph{Gaussian} label over the same $3\times3$ footprint with
weights taken from the measured point spread function. The positive-class loss weight was recomputed
per arm so that total positive gradient mass is held constant, which is what makes the arms
comparable.

The control is verified directly: retraining the flat arm reproduces the frozen
checkpoints bit-for-bit at epoch~\TinyEpochs{} on all eight seeds. \textbf{The control is the frozen
arm}, so any difference between arms is attributable to the label alone.

\textbf{The measured result.} The incumbent label costs \EthreeFlatDvRatio{} times the Doppler error
of a point label. The sharpest test was pre-registered as the growth of localisation error with
training, and it lands as the hypothesis predicted: between epoch~5 and epoch~\TinyEpochs{} Doppler
error grows by a factor of \EthreeFlatGrowth{} under flat supervision, \EthreeGaussGrowth{} under
the Gaussian and \EthreePointGrowth{} under the point label --- monotone in how flat the supervision
is. At the exact-cell gate the incumbent actually \emph{degrades} with training while the other two
hold, so flat supervision does not merely cap exact-cell accuracy; more training destroys it.

\textbf{What it supports.} \textbf{Most of the frozen arm's localisation deficit is attributable to
the supervision, and is largely removable.} Concentrating the label reduces Doppler error from
\EthreeFlatDv{} bins to \EthreeGaussDv{} (weighted) and \EthreePointDv{} (point), while the weighted
label moves detection by \EthreeGaussDeltaPd{} --- within seed noise. That converts a limitation of
the benchmark configuration into a design choice with a measured alternative, and it retires two
competing explanations: the deficit is not the network's capacity, and not the matching gate.

\textbf{Caveat.} The point label was pre-registered as likely to train poorly at its extreme class
imbalance, which would have vindicated the $3\times3$ choice. It trained stably on all
\TinySeeds{} seeds and localised best, so that prediction failed. The $3\times3$ label's only
advantage is \EthreePointPdGap{} of $\Pd$ at the primary gate, and even that is not uniform: the
point label is significantly worse on \EthreePointWorseConds{} of \EthreeConds{} conditions, all of
them clutter-bearing, where sparser supervision gives less gradient signal --- but significantly
\emph{better} on \EthreePointBetterConds{}, the two resolution-limited conditions, where a response
concentrated on one cell is what separates two targets that a broader one merges. The weighted label
avoids the cost without incurring either, and is the better choice on this evidence.

\section{Guard-masked patch network: is it information access?}

\textbf{What was tested.} Whether the network's freedom to use its guard cells --- the cells CFAR
must discard --- is what buys its detection advantage. Denying them turned out to be impossible on a
dense fully-convolutional network, and the reason is structural: with a guard of $\pm2$, the cell
one bin from a given cell under test lies inside that cell's guard region while being its own cell
under test, and one input map cannot hold it simultaneously hidden and visible. The masked arm must
therefore be a patch model centred on each cell under test. The primary contrast is consequently
\textbf{masked patch against unmasked patch}, so that the patch-training differences are common-mode
and cancel.

\textbf{The measured result. The two gates disagree, and that disagreement is the finding.}

At the primary gate guard access contributes, but the effect is small and not uniform: removing
those cells costs under two percentage points of $\Pd$ on average, the sign is positive on
\EfourPosOne{} of \EfourNOne{} conditions rather than all of them, and only \EfourSigOne{} reach
significance. At the exact-cell gate the sign reverses on every condition and the effect is far
larger --- on isolated targets the masked model reaches
\EfourIsoMasked{} against the unmasked model's \EfourIsoUnmasked{}, nearly double. Mainlobe energy
in the guard cells makes the network's response \emph{broad}: broad enough to match within one bin,
not sharp enough to identify the peak. Deny those cells and the response sharpens.

\textbf{What it supports.} The guard cells carry information the learned detector genuinely uses for
detection, but that information is not simply energy --- E2 integrated the same energy into CFAR and
it hurt. The network's use of those cells is also part of what makes it place targets imprecisely.

\textbf{Caveat, and it is a substantial one.} Both patch arms are undertrained relative to the dense
frozen model, because uniform sampling of cells under test gives far fewer positive examples per
epoch. This is common-mode, so the \emph{difference} between the arms is unbiased, but the absolute
$\Pd$ of both arms sits below the dense model's and should not be compared with
Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic}.

\section{Synthesis}

Taken together the three experiments narrow the field without closing it.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Energy integration is ruled out} as the explanation for the detection margin. Given
CFAR the same integrated energy, it performs worse, not better (E2).
\item \textbf{The frozen arm's placement behaviour is largely set by its supervision, and is
substantially fixable} (E3). Guard-region access broadens the response as well (E4), so two
different design choices push in the same direction.
\item \textbf{Guard-cell access buys detection, but little of it}, and it is paid for in placement
precision (E4).
\end{itemize}

The explanation these leave standing for the \emph{detection} margin is the learned decision rule
itself --- the network's ability to weight its receptive field in a way no fixed kernel reproduces
--- rather than any simple property of what it is given. That is a narrowing, not an identification,
and this chapter does not claim to have isolated a single cause.

One consequence carries into Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata}. Two different interventions produced the
same signature: a response broader than one cell, harmless at a tolerant gate and costly at a strict
one. The real-data tolerance ladder shows the same shape, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:litcompare} treats a broad response as \emph{consistent with} that behaviour rather
than as its established cause.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:attrib_scope}

Three pre-registered interventions were run, at zero cost to the frozen benchmark. The cheap
explanation for the detection margin was eliminated; the frozen arm's placement behaviour was
traced largely to its supervision and shown to be substantially fixable; and the value of
guard-region access was measured.

Two limits bound everything in this chapter.

\textbf{Validation split only.} No experiment here touched the held-out data, so none of these
results changes any number in Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic}. \textbf{This chapter explains that
benchmark; it does not replace it.}

\textbf{Interventions, not a general mechanism.} Each experiment removes or substitutes one thing
and reports what that costs. That is enough to eliminate a candidate explanation and enough to
locate a design choice, but it does not add up to a complete account of why the learned detector
behaves as it does, and no such account is claimed.

All three experiments are reported at the final suppression radius of \ProtocolNms{}. They were
first analysed at the superseded radius \ProtocolNmsOriginal{} and re-scored from the same frozen
checkpoints before submission, with no model retrained;
Appendix~\ref{app:attribrescore} records what that involved and what it did not change.

Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} now asks whether any of this survives contact with real radar
measurements.
