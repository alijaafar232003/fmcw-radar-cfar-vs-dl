\section*{Summary of Findings}

This project asked what a small learned local decision rule adds to radar target detection when it
is compared against CFAR on genuinely equal terms. Four results answer that.

\textbf{On matched synthetic data the learned detector is ahead everywhere, and clearly ahead where
CFAR is known to fail.} Across all \SynConditions{} scored conditions of a held-out split read once,
the frozen network's detection probability exceeded that of the best CFAR variant for each
condition, with the difference significant after Bonferroni correction in \SynWins{} of them. The
margin is not uniform. It is largest at clutter edges (\SynClutterEdgeDiff{}), which is precisely
the regime where a classical threshold is estimated from the wrong background, and it is not
established on the two strata where physics rather than the detector sets the limit. Expressed in
the currency the comparable literature uses, the network reaches half detection at a cell SNR
\SI{\SynSnrGainDL}{\deci\bel} below CA-CFAR --- a margin of ordinary size for this literature, not
an outlier.

\textbf{That advantage is bought at a measurable cost in localisation.} Pooled over all conditions
the network's combined placement error is \LocRmseRatio{} times CA-CFAR's, and \LocDvRatio{} times
worse on the Doppler axis. Scored on the exact cell rather than within one bin it does not reach
half detection at all. \textbf{The network detects more and places worse}, and both halves of that
sentence are properties of the same frozen system.

\textbf{The advantage is not explained by simple energy integration.} Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution}
tested the explanations rather than arguing for one, which narrows the range of plausible mechanisms
without claiming to have isolated a single cause.

\textbf{The synthetic advantage does not carry across a change of domain.} Evaluated zero-shot on
real automotive radar --- no retraining, no fine-tuning, no recalibration --- the two detectors
become statistically indistinguishable at exact annotated support. The sequence-balanced paired
difference is \RoneDelta{}, and its \SI{95}{\percent} interval $[\RoneCIlo, \RoneCIhi]$ includes
zero. The network is ahead at wider tolerance gates and behind in the nearest range block. It
reaches that comparable recall while emitting \RoneBurdenRatio{} times as many unmatched detections
per frame as CA-CFAR.

\section*{Answering the Research Question}

The three questions of Section~\ref{sec:problem} can now be answered separately, which was the point
of asking them separately.

\textbf{Is there a genuine advantage?} Yes, within the domain the detector was developed on, and it
survives a demanding protocol: comparable local context, matched \emph{measured} false-alarm rates,
a single evaluator, a per-condition best-CFAR comparator, eight seeds, and a tolerance ladder rather
than one gate. It is a real advantage and a modest one.

\textbf{Where does it come from?} Not from mainlobe energy integration, which was tested and
rejected as a sufficient explanation. The evidence points to the learned decision rule itself rather
than to a simple property of the network's input --- but the attribution narrows the field rather
than closing it, and this report does not claim a single identified cause.

\textbf{Does it survive outside the training domain?} No. The margin does not transfer. What is
worth stating carefully is that it is not \emph{replaced} by a symmetrical advantage for the
classical detector either: on real data the two are hard to separate on recall. The learned
detector did not fail; its advantage simply did not follow it out of the simulator.

That leads to the project's most useful conclusion, and it is not the one the synthetic result alone
would have suggested. \textbf{Detection probability is not sufficient to rank these detectors.} On
real extended-object data the two arms are separable on output density even where they are not
separable on recall, and the comparison proved sensitive to a post-processing constant --- the
suppression radius --- that the synthetic benchmark is almost entirely insensitive to. A learned
detector's practical value therefore depends on candidate density, localisation and post-processing
as much as on the score map it produces. Reporting $\Pd$ alone would have concealed all three.

\section*{Limitations}

The chapters state their own limitations in full; the ones that bound the project as a whole are
these.

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{One simulator and one real dataset.} The synthetic results describe one signal model,
and the external validation rests on a single sensor on a single test track. A second real dataset
could reinforce or overturn the transfer result.
\item \textbf{One architecture, one label representation, one training configuration.} Nothing here
supports a claim about learned detectors in general. The $3\times3$ block label in particular is a
known limitation, retained because changing it would have invalidated every measurement made with
it.
\item \textbf{The real-data comparison is one-sided.} CARRADA supports no false-alarm measurement,
so the network's larger output cannot be priced against its recall. The central trade-off of
Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} is therefore bounded rather than resolved.
\item \textbf{No causal intervention across domains.} The domain audit locates differences; it does
not establish that any of them caused the loss of the margin.
\item \textbf{The post-processing protocol was revised during the work}, on synthetic development
data and after an earlier version of the real-data experiment had been run.
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} records exactly what changed and what did not.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Future Work}

Four directions follow from the evidence rather than from ambition.

\textbf{Vary the label representation.} The localisation deficit and the tolerance behaviour on
real data both point at a response broader than a single cell. The label and the receptive field are
independent design axes and neither was varied here.

\textbf{Test the transfer result on a second real dataset.} A single measurement is not a norm, and
no published zero-shot synthetic-to-real result exists in this family to compare it against.

\textbf{Establish a mechanism by intervention.} Training on data whose background statistics are
deliberately modified, and observing the consequence, would convert the domain audit's correlations
into a causal statement. That is a different experiment, not an extension of this one.

\textbf{Treat candidate extraction as part of the detector.} The suppression radius was found to
matter on extended real objects and not on synthetic point targets. A detector intended for real
extended objects should be evaluated with its post-processing varied, not held fixed at a value
chosen on point targets.
