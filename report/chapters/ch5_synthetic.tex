\ulfgchapter{5}{Synthetic Benchmark Results}{chp:synthetic}

\section{Introduction}

This chapter reports what the protocol of Chapter~\ref{chp:detectors} returned when applied to the
benchmark of Chapter~\ref{chp:simulator}. It is a results chapter: it states what was measured, and
leaves the question of \emph{why} to Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} and the question of whether it
generalises to Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata}.

Four measurements are reported, in the order that makes them interpretable.

\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Was the operating point actually matched?} A comparison at a supposedly common
false-alarm rate means nothing until that is verified, so it is checked first.
\item \textbf{The headline result}: detection probability per condition, with confidence intervals
and a correction for multiple comparisons.
\item \textbf{The same result in decibels}, which is the currency the comparable literature of
Section~\ref{sec:sota} reports, and which makes the margin externally interpretable.
\item \textbf{What the headline does not show}: localisation, tolerance sensitivity and
computational cost, each of which qualifies the result in a different direction.
\end{enumerate}

Every number below comes from a single read of one held-out split. That split
(\SynHoldoutFrames{} frames, \SynFramesPerCondition{} in each of its \SynHoldoutStrata{} strata,
of which \SynScoredFrames{} contain targets and are scored for detection) was generated after the
configuration and the evaluation protocol were both fixed, from a seed never previously used, and
was verified to share no frame content with the training, validation, tuning or SNR-sweep data. It
had not been scored by anything before the results in this chapter were produced.
Appendix~\ref{app:prereg} records why a new split was generated rather than an earlier one
re-scored.

\section{The operating point was matched}

Chapter~\ref{chp:detectors} argued that matching at design $\Pfa$ is not sufficient, because the
achieved rate and the design rate differ, and differ by a different amount for each variant. That
argument is only useful if the matching then worked.

Figure~\ref{fig:oppoint} shows the achieved per-cell false-alarm rate for every arm and every
condition, as a ratio to the design rate of $10^{-4}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/Chapter5/ch5_fig5_operating_point.png}

for all five arms across every condition of the held-out split, including the target-free

\label{fig:oppoint}
\end{figure}

Every value lies between \PfaRatioMin{} and \PfaRatioMax{} --- within two per cent of unity. The
comparison that follows is therefore at a genuinely common measured operating point, not at an
assumed one.

its whole usable range, pooled over the seven scored conditions.

\section{Detection probability by condition}

Figure~\ref{fig:headline} gives the headline measurement: detection probability for the frozen
network against the best CFAR variant for each condition, at the primary $\pm\ProtocolGate$-bin
gate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter5/ch5_fig1_headline.png}
\caption[Detection probability by condition]{Left: detection probability at matched measured
$\Pfa$. Right: the paired difference with its \SI{95}{\percent} bootstrap confidence interval.
The classical arm is the best-performing CFAR variant for that condition, which is a deliberately
demanding comparator.}
\label{fig:headline}
\end{figure}

Two features of the comparison deserve emphasis before the numbers.

\textbf{The classical arm is the best variant per condition.} Rather than fixing one variant
throughout, each condition is scored against whichever of CA, GO, SO or OS performs best there.
This is a stronger baseline than any single detector could achieve in deployment, because it
amounts to an oracle choosing the right variant for a condition it has already seen. The margin
reported is therefore a conservative one.

\textbf{Eight seeds, not one.} Every network value is the mean over \TinySeeds{} independently
trained seeds.

Read practically rather than by sign count, the result is: \textbf{\SynWins{} established gains, one
small difference on the unresolved stratum that does not survive correction, and a practical tie on
homogeneous clutter.} Every difference happens to carry a positive sign, but two of the seven are
too small to be claimed, and reporting the result as ``ahead in all seven'' would rest on a
\SynClutterDiff{} margin that means nothing. Among the \SynWins{} established differences the margin
ranges from \SynMultiDiff{} on multi-target scenes to \SynClutterEdgeDiff{} at clutter edges, a
factor of more than eight between the smallest and the largest.

That pattern is itself informative, and it is consistent with the failure modes of
Section~\ref{sec:cfar_failures}.

\textbf{The largest margin is where CFAR is documented to fail.} The clutter-edge condition ---
target in clean noise with the reference window reaching into the ridge --- is where the classical
threshold is set from the wrong background. The network gains \SynClutterEdgeDiff{} there, roughly
three times its next largest margin.

The two unestablished conditions are unestablished for different reasons.

\textbf{Unresolved.} The unresolved-pair condition is primarily a protocol and physics sanity check:
it places two truths inside a single resolvable response, so the intended detection ceiling is near
$\Pd = 0.5$ for either arm. Its small TinyCNN--CFAR difference (\SynUnresolvedDiff{},
$p = \SynUnresolvedP{}$) does not survive the Bonferroni correction, so no detector advantage is
claimed.

\textbf{Homogeneous clutter.} In homogeneous clutter the two approaches are effectively tied at the
primary operating point (\SynClutterDiff{}, $p = \SynClutterP{}$).

\section{The result in decibels}

Per-condition probabilities are hard to compare against published work, which reports margins in
decibels of signal-to-noise ratio. Figure~\ref{fig:pdsnr} expresses the same frozen result in that
currency, using the dedicated SNR-sweep split.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter5/ch5_fig2_pd_vs_snr.png}
\caption[Detection probability against cell SNR]{Both empirical detectors against the clairvoyant
reference. Detection probability as a function of measured cell SNR at matched measured $\Pfa$;
TinyCNN and CA-CFAR are drawn solid, the secondary CFAR variants dotted, and the dashed curve is a
clairvoyant detector that knows the true noise power. Left, the primary gate: TinyCNN reaches

comparison is like for like: CA-CFAR crosses at \SI{\SynSnrFiftyExactCA}{\deci\bel},

$\Pd = 0.5$.}
\label{fig:pdsnr}

The same result can be expressed as an SNR margin. TinyCNN reaches $\Pd = 0.5$ at
\SI{\SynSnrFiftyDL}{\deci\bel}, compared with \SI{\SynSnrFiftyCA}{\deci\bel} for CA-CFAR, giving a
\SI{\SynSnrGainDL}{\deci\bel} advantage at this detection level. The corresponding CA-CFAR
exact-cell crossing lies \SI{\SynCfarLossCA}{\deci\bel} above the clairvoyant reference, indicating
that CFAR loss explains only part of the observed margin.

The right-hand panel carries one qualification. Scored on the exact cell rather than within one bin,
the network never reaches $\Pd = 0.5$, peaking at \SynPdMaxExactDL{}, while every CFAR variant
crosses it. \textbf{The network's advantage exists at the $\pm1$-bin gate and reverses at the exact
cell}, which points to localisation without isolating it.
Section~\ref{sec:localization} measures placement directly, and Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} tests
the mechanism.

Figure~\ref{fig:tol} does so.

\caption[Sensitivity to matching tolerance]{The paired difference at the primary
$\pm\ProtocolGate$-bin gate and at the $\pm\ProtocolGateRobust$-bin robustness gate, for every

In six of the seven conditions the ordering is unchanged and the magnitude moves little; widening
the gate slightly \emph{increases} the network's margin on most of them, and the unresolved
stratum, marginal at the primary gate, becomes significant at the wider one. The conclusion that
the network leads on those conditions does not depend on the tolerance chosen, which is what the
robustness gate was included to establish.

The exception is homogeneous clutter, where the difference moves from \SynClutterDiff{} at the
primary gate to \SynTolThreeClutterDiff{} at the wider one, changing sign. The bootstrap $p$-value
there is \SynTolThreeClutterP{}, nowhere near the corrected significance threshold, so this report
does not claim the classical arm wins in that condition either. It is a second indication that
localisation contributes to the observed difference --- not an independent one, since both
indications come from the same detections on the same frames.
Section~\ref{sec:localization} measures placement directly.

\section{Localisation}
\label{sec:localization}

Both previous sections pointed to the same place, so it is measured directly. Localisation error is
computed over true positives only --- it asks, of the targets a detector did find, how precisely it
placed them.

That definition carries a caveat which should be read before the numbers. \textbf{Each detector's
error is averaged over its own true positives, so the two averages are not taken over the same set
of targets.} The network detects more targets, and the extra ones it finds are on average the
harder, weaker targets that CFAR missed; those are exactly the targets whose peaks are least well
localised. Part of the difference reported below is therefore a difference in which targets are
being averaged, not only in how well each arm places them. The metric is also bounded by the gate:
a detection displaced further than the gate is scored as a miss rather than as a large error, so
severe displacement appears as lost $\Pd$ instead. Both gates are reported for that reason, and
Figure~\ref{fig:loc} gives the measured errors.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter5/ch5_fig4_localization.png}
\caption[Localisation error]{Mean absolute localisation error by axis, and combined RMSE, pooled
over all conditions at the primary gate. Measured over true positives only.}
\label{fig:loc}
\end{figure}

Pooled over all conditions, the frozen network's combined RMSE is \LocRmseDL{} bins against
\LocRmseCA{} for CA-CFAR --- \LocRmseRatio{} times larger. Broken down by axis, the range error is
\LocDrDL{} bins against \LocDrCA{}, and the Doppler error is \LocDvDL{} bins against \LocDvCA{}, a
factor of \LocDvRatio{}. In physical units the network places detections to
\SI{\LocRangeMDL}{\metre} and \SI{\LocVelMpsDL}{\metre\per\second} against CA-CFAR's
\SI{\LocRangeMCA}{\metre} and \SI{\LocVelMpsCA}{\metre\per\second}.

All four CFAR variants localise similarly well, between \LocRmseCFARMin{} and \LocRmseCFARMax{}
bins. That the four classical arms agree with one another and the learned arm sits outside their
range indicates that this is a property of the learned arm as configured here, rather than of the
evaluation.

\textbf{This is a measured cost of the benchmark configuration, and it should be scoped carefully.}
It is a property of \emph{this} frozen arm, trained with the $3\times3$ block label of
Section~\ref{sec:labels} --- not a general property of learned detection. A block label asks the
network to respond over a region rather than at one cell, which is precisely the behaviour measured
here. Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} varies the label directly and finds that most of this gap is
attributable to that choice and is largely removable: supervision concentrated on the target cell
reduces the error substantially at essentially no cost in detection. The figure above therefore
records what the frozen benchmark does, not a limit on what a small convolutional detector can do.

\section{Computational cost}

Detection probability is not the only axis on which these detectors differ.
Table~\ref{tab:runtime} gives the measured cost per frame.

\midrule
CA-CFAR & \SI{\RuntimeCA}{\milli\second} & cheapest; a box sum per cell \\
TinyCNN & \SI{\RuntimeDL}{\milli\second} & \TinyParams{} parameters, \RuntimeMMac{} MMAC/frame \\
GO-CFAR & \SI{\RuntimeGO}{\milli\second} & two half-window sums, then a maximum \\

\bottomrule

The ordering is worth noting because it cuts both ways, and because the intuition it violates is a
common one. The network performs roughly three orders of magnitude more arithmetic per cell than
CA-CFAR, and it is \RuntimeDLoverCA{} times slower --- but it is faster than three of the four
classical variants, and \RuntimeOSoverDL{} times faster than OS-CFAR. The reason is not that the
network is cheap; it is that CFAR here is vectorised, so a box mean costs the same regardless of
window size, whereas a per-cell rank selection does not vectorise at all. \textbf{A learned
detector is therefore not automatically the expensive option}: it is expensive relative to the
simplest classical rule and cheap relative to the more robust ones.

\section{Conclusion}

On the synthetic benchmark, measured at a verified common operating point:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{The network establishes a gain on the five resolvable challenge conditions} ---
isolated, masking, multi-target, mixed and clutter edge --- after correction for multiple
comparisons, against a per-condition best-CFAR comparator stronger than any deployable single
variant. The margin is largest where CFAR is documented to fail, reaching
\SynClutterEdgeDiff{} at clutter edges.
\item \textbf{Homogeneous clutter is a practical tie} (\SynClutterDiff), and the difference moves
against the network when the matching tolerance is widened, without approaching significance.
\item \textbf{The unresolved stratum is a resolution and protocol stress test rather than an
ordinary competitive condition.} Both arms sit near the ceiling its construction imposes, and the
small difference does not survive correction.
\item In the currency the comparable literature uses, the network reaches half detection at a cell
SNR \SI{\SynSnrGainDL}{\deci\bel} lower than CA-CFAR.
\item \textbf{This frozen arm places its detections less precisely than CFAR}
(\LocRmseRatio{} times the combined RMSE, \LocDvRatio{} times on the Doppler axis), and at the
exact-cell gate it does not reach half detection. That is a measured property of the $3\times3$
block label this arm was trained with, not of learned detection in general:
Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} varies the label and removes most of the gap.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{This establishes a controlled in-domain advantage, not real-world superiority.} A learned
local decision rule \emph{can} improve on CFAR under controlled conditions --- that is a statement
about potential, measured on the simulator that produced the training data, and nothing in this
chapter licenses more than it.

The question the benchmark leaves open is whether that improvement is a property of the detector or
of the distribution it was trained on. The next chapters take one piece each:
Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} asks where the margin comes from and how much of the placement
behaviour is set by supervision; Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} freezes the detectors and asks whether
any of it survives contact with real radar measurements; and Chapter~\ref{chp:domaingap} asks what
would have to change for it to.
