\section{Introduction}

Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata} ended on a negative result and a measurement. The negative result is that
the synthetic margin did not survive transfer: frozen on real data, the learned detector and CA-CFAR
became statistically indistinguishable in exact-support recall while the learned arm emitted roughly
twice the output. The measurement is the domain audit, which found the simulator and the real sensor
to differ in background smoothness, spread, ridge structure and range behaviour by margins far larger
than any tuning constant in the project.

Those two things leave exactly two questions open, and this chapter answers one each.

\begin{description}
\itemsep3pt
\item[Question 1 --- the training distribution.] Was the transfer loss caused partly by the
training distribution? If the synthetic \emph{training} domain is made statistically closer to the
real one, does transfer improve --- \emph{without any real radar frame entering neural
optimisation}? \textbf{Stage~4} addresses this.
\item[Question 2 --- real supervision.] Can real supervision turn recovered recall into an efficient
operating point? If real supervision is finally permitted, can the same
$\TinyParams$-parameter network exceed CA-CFAR on real sequences at a comparable output burden?
\textbf{Stage~5} addresses this, and is the first and only point in the thesis where a real radar
frame reaches a gradient.
\end{description}

\textbf{The status of these two experiments, stated plainly.} Neither is a fresh zero-shot external
validation, and neither is presented as one. Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata}'s evaluation was the single
planned read of the CARRADA test split; everything here comes afterwards and is reported as
follow-up work. The chronology is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}.

\section{Stage 4: domain-informed synthetic training}
\label{sec:stage4}

\subsection{What is being isolated}

The domain audit of Section~\ref{sec:r7audit} measured differences, not causes. Stage~4 turns those
measurements into an intervention: the synthetic background is rebuilt so that its \emph{measured}
statistics move toward the real ones, and everything else is held fixed. If transfer improves, the
training distribution mattered. If it does not, the loss of the margin lies elsewhere.

The generator's parameters were derived from the \textbf{18 official CARRADA training sequences and
nothing else}. The validation split was not used, and the test split was not opened by any code that
influenced the design. Three background properties were changed, all of them set from train-derived
measurements:

\begin{itemize}
\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Multi-look-like smoothness.} The original chain produces a single-look background: its
measured spread is \SI{5.569}{\decibel} against the closed-form single-look value of
\SI{5.570}{\decibel}. The real background is consistent with an effective Gamma shape near seven to
eight, so frames are formed as a power average of several independent background realisations
sharing one deterministic target field.
\item \textbf{Range-dependent mean background.} The measured train range profile, unit-mean in
power, is applied to the background alone --- never to the targets, whose amplitudes come from the
frozen radar equation.
\item \textbf{A narrow, persistent zero-Doppler ridge.} Strong, about one bin wide at half power,
and present in essentially every frame rather than in the minority of frames the original strata
produced.
\end{itemize}

The target model, the point spread function, the range and Doppler windows, the labels, the target
placement, the SNR sampling and the dataset sizes are untouched. Only the background changed.

\subsection{What could not be matched, and why}

This is a \textbf{partial domain-gap reduction}, not a reproduction of CARRADA. Two mismatches
survive deliberately, and both trace to one frozen constraint. The lag-1 Doppler correlation of the
real background is far lower than the simulator's. That correlation is set by the slow-time window,
and the same window defines the target point spread function, the measured mainlobe width and hence
the guard geometry that makes the CFAR comparison fair. Changing it would change the detector
contract established in Chapter~\ref{chp:detectors}, so it was left alone and declared. The same
constraint widens the synthetic ridge's skirt relative to the real one, whose fall-off is sharper
than any window of this family permits. Both residuals were recorded before any model was trained.

\subsection{Result on the CARRADA test split}

Eight seeds were trained on the revised synthetic domain under the frozen training contract ---
same architecture, optimiser, learning rate, batch size, epoch count, positive weighting and flat
$3\times3$ label --- and thresholds were derived from the new synthetic validation split alone.

\CiTestNearNew{} against CA-CFAR's \CiTestNearCFAR{} --- the deficit closed.

\subsection{Recall--output trade-off}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The recall gain was bought with output density.} Unmatched detections rose from
\CiTestBurdenOrig{} to \CiTestBurdenNew{} per frame, a factor of \CiTestBurdenRatio{}, against
CA-CFAR's \CiTestBurdenCFAR{}.
\end{quote}

A detector that fires more often recovers more objects, so a burden-matched diagnostic was run to ask
whether anything survived once output was equalised. Two things did, and they point in opposite
directions. The informed arm beats the original arm at \emph{equal} output, so the gain is not merely
density. But CA-CFAR leads \emph{both} learned arms at every matched burden level.

\textbf{Supported conclusion.} Reducing the measured synthetic-to-real background mismatch
substantially changed transfer behaviour and recovered much of the learned detector's real-data
recall, without any real frame entering neural optimisation. It did \textbf{not} establish
superiority over CA-CFAR once detector output burden is controlled.

\subsection{What this stage can and cannot attribute}

Several background properties changed together. The experiment supports a causal statement about the
\emph{combined} intervention --- changing the synthetic training domain toward the measured target
statistics changed transfer performance --- and nothing narrower. It does not establish that the
smoothness, the ridge or the range profile was individually responsible. Separating them would need
one ablation per property, which the General Conclusion lists as future work and this thesis does not
claim to have run.

\section{Stage 5: supervised real-domain adaptation}
\label{sec:stage5}

Stage~4 recovered recall and left an efficiency problem. Stage~5 asks the question the whole thesis
has been circling: with real supervision finally permitted, can the same small network exceed the
classical baseline at a comparable output burden?

\subsection{Protocol}

Fine-tuning starts from each of the eight Stage-4 checkpoints, preserving seed identity, and uses
\textbf{CARRADA training sequences only}. The 18 training sequences were divided at \textbf{sequence
level} into \FtFitSeq{} for fitting and \FtDevSeq{} for internal development; frames from one
recording never straddle the two sides, because consecutive frames of a sequence are near-duplicates
and a frame-level split would leak one into the other almost perfectly.

\textbf{The supervision changes here, and that is the point.} The synthetic arms were trained on flat
$3\times3$ blocks around point targets. Fine-tuning uses the \textbf{provided dense segmentation
masks}, restricted to the detector-valid interior, with annotation-empty frames kept as genuine
all-background examples. Nothing is invented: no centroids, no brightest cells, no bounding-box
centres. This is therefore real-domain \emph{adaptation}, not a continuation of the original
point-target task.

All layers train, including the normalisation statistics --- a $\TinyParams$-parameter network has no
sub-network worth freezing. The architecture is untouched. The configuration is deliberately small:
\num{1e-4} learning rate, ten epochs at most, early stopping on internal-development loss, positive
weight \FtPosWeight{} fixed by a rule declared before the class balance was measured.

\textbf{The operating point was fixed on training data, before the evaluation split was opened, and
it is part of what Stage~5 changes.} CARRADA cannot support a false-alarm measurement, so the
threshold cannot be set by a measured $\Pfa$ as it is on synthetic data. Instead, CA-CFAR was run on
the three internal-development sequences and its output burden measured (\FtCaDevBurden{} unmatched
detections per frame); each seed's threshold is the one maximising exact recall on those sequences
subject to not exceeding that burden. This is a \textbf{burden-constrained operating-point rule}
using training data alone. It is stated explicitly because the frozen operating points of Stage~4 and
Stage~5 were chosen by different rules, and Section~\ref{sec:selectivity} depends on that
distinction.

The official CARRADA validation split had never been scored by any detector, nor used for any model
or threshold decision. It was read once, after the configuration above was frozen.

Substantial sequence-level uncertainty remains, and a different six sequences could give a different
interval.

\subsection{Fine-tuning improves selectivity rather than recall}
\label{sec:selectivity}

Stage~5 is not a further recall improvement, and reading it as one would misstate what changed.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Against the Stage-4 arm, fine-tuning established no recall gain:} \FtVsCiDelta{}, 95\,\% CI
[\FtVsCiCIlo{}, \FtVsCiCIhi{}], an interval that includes zero.
\end{quote}

What changed is the \emph{shape} of the recall--output trade-off, and the evidence for that is the
burden curve of Figure~\ref{fig:burdencurve} rather than the frozen operating points alone. At
CA-CFAR's own \FtMatchedBurden{} unmatched detections per frame, the fine-tuned arm reads
\FtMatchedFt{} against the CARRADA-informed synthetic arm's \FtMatchedCi{}, the original synthetic
arm's \FtMatchedOrig{} and CA-CFAR's \FtMatchedCFAR{}. That comparison holds output constant, so it
isolates the trade-off from the threshold choice: \textbf{real-domain adaptation substantially
improved the learned detector's recall--output trade-off.}

The change at the \emph{frozen} operating points --- \FtValBurdenCi{} to \FtValBurdenFt{} unmatched
detections per frame at equal recall --- is larger than the weight update alone accounts for, because
Stage~4 and Stage~5 thresholds were chosen by different rules. The burden-constrained
operating-point selection of Section~\ref{sec:stage5} is what converted the improved trade-off into a
deployment-relevant operating point; the sweep in Figure~\ref{fig:burdencurve} is what shows the
trade-off itself improved. Both steps are part of Stage~5, and neither on its own produces the
result.

Two further observations, both pre-specified. The near-range block continues to improve, from
\FtNearOrig{} through \FtNearCi{} to \FtNearFt{} against CA-CFAR's \FtNearCFAR{}. And pedestrians,
the most numerous and hardest class, move from \FtPedOrig{} through \FtPedCi{} to \FtPedFt{} against
CA-CFAR's \FtPedCFAR{}. Class recall is confounded by mask size --- larger objects offer more support
cells to hit --- so these are reported as strata, not as class-wise capability claims.

Seed spread also collapses along the sequence: \FtSeedSDOrig{} for the original arm, \FtSeedSDCi{}
after domain-informed training, \FtSeedSDFt{} after fine-tuning. Real supervision made the eight
independently seeded networks behave far more alike.

\subsection{Limitations of the fine-tuning result}
\label{sec:stage5limits}

\textbf{The comparison gives the learned detector something CA-CFAR does not get.} The fine-tuned
network is supervised with the same dense segmentation support that the primary metric scores
against. That is legitimate supervised adaptation --- it is exactly what the experiment set out to
test --- but it aligns the network's output geometry with the truth geometry, and the classical
detector has no equivalent opportunity. The defensible statement is therefore \emph{under supervised
target-domain adaptation, the learned detector outperformed CA-CFAR on this evaluation}, not that it
is inherently the better detector.

\textbf{The fine-tune is deliberately small, and was still improving when it stopped.} Every seed
reached the ten-epoch ceiling with its development loss still falling; early stopping never
triggered. The result is a lower bound on what this configuration can do, not a converged one.

\textbf{Six sequences bound the inference.} The statistical caveat recorded with the primary result
applies to every comparison in this section.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:domaingapconclusion}

Two follow-up experiments, each answering the question the previous stage left open.

Target-domain-informed synthetic training showed that the synthetic training distribution was a
material part of the transfer failure: recalibrating the background toward measured target-domain
statistics recovered a large part of the lost real-data recall \emph{without any real frame entering
neural optimisation}. It also showed that recall is not a sufficient summary, because the recovery
came with roughly \CiTestBurdenRatio{} times the output density and did not survive burden matching
against the classical baseline.

Supervised real-domain adaptation then produced the strongest learned-versus-classical result in this
thesis: higher exact recall than CA-CFAR at a slightly lower output burden, on sequences no detector
in this project had previously scored. Its contribution was not recall --- that had already been
recovered --- but the recall--output trade-off, which improved enough for a burden-constrained
operating point chosen on training data to land above the classical baseline on both axes at once.

Taken together these two stages convert Chapter~\ref{chp:realdata}'s negative result into a
conditional one. The learned detector's advantage was never a fixed property of the architecture. It
was a property of how well its training distribution matched deployment, and it became an advantage
over the classical baseline only once the deployment domain was represented during training --- first
statistically, then with real supervision.
