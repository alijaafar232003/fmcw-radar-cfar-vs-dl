\section{Introduction}

This chapter specifies the two detectors compared in this report and fixes the rules by which they
are scored. Both are specified before any comparative result appears, because a protocol chosen
after seeing results is not a measurement.

The chapter is organised around the fairness problem set out in Section~\ref{sec:problem}. Each of
the asymmetries listed there is addressed by a specific choice recorded here: the network's
receptive field is matched to the CFAR window; both detectors are reduced to the same output type
and scored by one code path; operating points are matched at measured rather than design
false-alarm rate; and results are reported across a range of matching tolerances rather than at a
single one.

One point of vocabulary is worth fixing at the outset. The two arms are not two different
\emph{systems}; they are two ways of obtaining the same kind of local decision rule, as
Section~\ref{sec:cfar_as_conv} established. Everything in this chapter follows from trying to hold
everything else constant so that the decision rule is what varies.

\section{The classical arm}

\subsection{Window geometry}

CFAR requires a reference window, and its shape is a design choice. The window used throughout this
report spans \CfarWinR{} bins in range by \CfarWinD{} in Doppler. Of its \CfarWinCells{} cells,
\textbf{1} is the cell under test, \textbf{\CfarNGuard{}} are guard cells excluded from the
estimate, and \textbf{\CfarNRef{}} are reference cells forming the background estimate $Z$.
Figure~\ref{fig:window} shows the layout.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/Chapter4/ch4_fig1_window.png}
\caption[The CFAR reference window used]{The reference window used by every CFAR arm in this
report. Counting outward from the centre, the range axis has \CfarGuardR{} guard cells then
\CfarTrainR{} reference cells on each side; the Doppler axis has \CfarGuardD{} and \CfarTrainD{}.}
\label{fig:window}
\end{figure}

Two aspects of that geometry were decided by measurement rather than convention.

\textbf{The guard count follows the measured point spread function.} Chapter~\ref{chp:simulator}
measured a mainlobe spanning about two bins. The guard ring must cover a target's own spread, or
the target inflates its own threshold and suppresses itself, so \CfarGuardR{} guard cells per side
is the smallest value that does the job.

\textbf{The window is rectangular because the two axes are not alike.} The range axis has
\RadarRangeBins{} bins and no large-scale structure; the Doppler axis has only \RadarM{} and
carries the zero-Doppler clutter ridge. A window sized for range would span a third of the Doppler
axis and drag ridge energy across a wide band of thresholds. Figure~\ref{fig:geomsweep} shows the
candidate geometries evaluated on the dedicated tuning split.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/Chapter4/ch4_fig3_geometry_sweep.png}
\caption[Window geometry selection]{Mean detection probability across conditions for each candidate
window, measured on \texttt{bench2\_tune}. The selected geometry was fixed before any read of the
test split.}
\label{fig:geomsweep}
\end{figure}

The selection was made on \texttt{bench2\_tune}, a split used for nothing else. This matters
because the network's operating point is selected on \texttt{bench2\_val}: if both selections used
the same data, one arm would effectively be tuned on the other's selection set.

\subsection{Variants}

Four variants are evaluated, differing only in how the reference cells become $Z$: CA, GO, SO and
OS, as described in Section~\ref{sec:cfar_failures}. OS uses a rank fraction of \CfarOsRank{}.
That fraction was never swept, which is a stated limitation: a better-tuned OS might do better than
the one reported here.

\subsection{Threshold calibration}
\label{sec:alphacal}

The multiplier $\alpha$ that achieves a chosen false-alarm rate is available in closed form for
CA-CFAR, as equation~\eqref{eq:alpha_ca}. It is not used here, and the reason is worth stating
because it is one of the asymmetries Section~\ref{sec:problem} identified.

All four multipliers are instead obtained by Monte-Carlo calibration. Pure-noise frames are passed
through the identical processing chain, the CFAR ratio map is computed for each, the ratio values
are pooled over all validly tested cells, and the threshold is taken as the $(1 - \Pfa)$ empirical
quantile of that pooled distribution. The result is the level exceeded by exactly a fraction $\Pfa$
of noise cells, by construction.

Figure~\ref{fig:alphacal} shows both the calibration itself and why it was necessary.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter4/ch4_fig2_alpha_calibration.png}
\caption[Threshold calibration]{Left: measured false-alarm rate against threshold multiplier for
each variant, on pure-noise frames through the identical chain; the marker is the calibrated value.
Right: the rate each variant actually achieves at the closed-form multiplier against the calibrated
one, measured on a verification seed not used for calibration.}
\label{fig:alphacal}
\end{figure}

The right-hand panel is the argument. At the closed-form multiplier the achieved rates are not the
design rate: CA overshoots it by a factor of about two and SO by more than six, while OS
undershoots by nearly an order of magnitude. After calibration all four sit at the design rate on
data not used to set them.

The CA row deserves a comment. The calibrated multiplier is about \SI{10}{\percent} higher than the
closed form, which corresponds to roughly \SI{0.4}{\deci\bel} of extra threshold. The closed form
assumes the \CfarNRef{} reference cells are statistically independent, whereas the window functions
of Chapter~\ref{chp:simulator} correlate neighbouring cells of the range--Doppler map. The
effective number of independent samples is therefore smaller than \CfarNRef{}, the estimate of $Z$
is noisier than the formula assumes, and a higher threshold is needed to hold the rate. This is the
CFAR loss of Section~\ref{sec:cfar_loss} appearing as a measurable quantity rather than a
hypothesis, and Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} returns to it.

\section{The learned arm}

\subsection{Architecture}

The learned detector, referred to throughout as TinyCNN, is a fully convolutional network that maps
a range--Doppler map to a per-cell score at the same resolution. It has \TinyParams{} parameters.
Figure~\ref{fig:network} shows its structure.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter4/ch4_fig4_network.png}
\caption[The frozen network]{Four dilated convolutional layers with the receptive field growing to
match the CFAR window. There is no downsampling, so the output remains at full range--Doppler
resolution and no peak is displaced.}
\label{fig:network}
\end{figure}

Three choices define it.

\textbf{The receptive field is matched to the CFAR window.} This is the central design decision of
the project. The layers use dilations $(\TinyDilR)$ along range and $(\TinyDilD)$ along Doppler,
which compose to a receptive field of $\TinyRFR \times \TinyRFD$ --- exactly the window of
Figure~\ref{fig:window}. Dilation is used because it enlarges the receptive field without adding
parameters and without downsampling. The value was verified empirically, by back-propagating from a
single output cell and recording which input cells receive non-zero gradient, rather than trusting
the arithmetic.

\textbf{There is no downsampling.} Pooling and upsampling would risk displacing the peak cell, and
a one-bin displacement degrades the localisation metric. Padding equals dilation at every layer, so
the spatial size is preserved exactly.

\textbf{The network is deliberately small.} At \TinyParams{} parameters and \TinyWidth{} channels
it is tiny by modern standards. That is intentional: the question is what a small learned local
rule adds over a fixed one, not how well a large network can be made to perform.

\subsection{What ``matched'' does and does not mean}
\label{sec:matched}

The receptive-field match is the fairness argument of this report, so its limits should be stated
plainly rather than discovered by a reader.

\textbf{Matching the footprint does not equalise the decision rule.} Both detectors see the same
$\TinyRFR \times \TinyRFD$ neighbourhood, but they use it differently. CFAR takes the cell under
test as its statistic, excludes the guard ring entirely, and estimates the background from the
\CfarNRef{} reference cells. The network has no such partition: all \CfarWinCells{} cells in its
footprint are available to it, and it may combine them in any way its weights encode.

\textbf{That asymmetry is a finding, not an oversight.} CFAR excludes the guard cells out of
necessity, because a simple mean cannot distinguish a bright neighbour caused by a target's own
mainlobe from a bright neighbour caused by high noise. A learned function can in principle make
that distinction, and so can exploit information the classical rule is forced to discard. The
correct statement is therefore: \emph{the same window, but CFAR is constrained to a hand-derived
statistic that requires discarding the inner cells}. Chapter~\ref{chp:attribution} treats this as
one of the candidate mechanisms.

\textbf{The nominal receptive field is not the effective one.} A convolutional network's influence
decays from the centre outward, because central cells contribute through many more paths than edge
cells. CFAR weights all its reference cells uniformly. The network's usable context is therefore
somewhat tighter than the nominal match suggests.

\subsection{Input representation}

The input transform is the highest-leverage preprocessing choice, and it is applied identically at
training and inference. Each map is converted to decibels relative to \emph{its own} noise floor,
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{1}{20}\,\mathrm{clip}\!\left(10 \log_{10}
\frac{P}{\operatorname{median}(P)},\; -10,\; +60\right),
\end{equation}
where $P$ is the range--Doppler power map. Normalising by the frame's own median rather than by its
maximum is deliberate: peak normalisation would rescale every frame by its brightest target and
destroy the absolute signal-to-noise information the detector needs.

\subsection{Labels}
\label{sec:labels}

Section~\ref{sec:sota} noted that a learned detector must be given a training target and that the
choice is not neutral. The label used here is a $3\times3$ block centred on each target's cell.

This is a known limitation and is recorded as such. A block label asks the network to respond over
a small region rather than at one exact cell, which relaxes the localisation demand relative to a
single-cell label. It is the leading suspect for the localisation behaviour reported in
Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic}, where the network's exact-cell performance is weaker than its
tolerant-gate performance. It was retained because changing it after the experimental record was
established would invalidate every result in that record; the alternative representations remain an
open axis, noted in the General Conclusion.

Three of those entries need explanation.

\textbf{Epoch selection.} The reported epoch was not chosen by early stopping. Validation loss
plateaus later than detection probability does, so stopping on loss selects the wrong epoch. Every
epoch from 1 to 25 was instead swept, and the reported epoch is the earliest whose pooled
seed-mean $\Pd$ lies within one standard error of the plateau --- a rule fixed before the sweep was
run.

\textbf{Positive weighting.} The positive class is vastly outnumbered, and exact class balancing
would imply a weight near 974. That value was tried and produced a result that later had to be
retracted; the weight of \TinyPosWeight{} won a three-way ablation on the validation split. The
retraction is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:corrections}.

\textbf{Thread count is load-bearing.} This is unusual enough to state explicitly. CPU tensor
kernels reduce in a different order at a different thread count, so the same seed produces
different weights: the maximum weight difference across the \TinyParams{} parameters is
$0.38$, which is about one standard error on localisation and negligible on $\Pd$. Runs trained at
different thread counts are therefore not pooled or compared. Fixing the thread count is part of
the reproducibility contract, not a performance setting.

\textbf{Eight seeds are reported, always.} A single training run is one sample from a distribution,
and the spread between seeds is not negligible. Every network result in this report is a mean over
\TinySeeds{} seeds with the individual seeds shown.

\section{The evaluation protocol}

\subsection{One rulebook}

Both detectors produce a score map: a ratio map for CFAR, a logit map for the network. Both are
then reduced to the same output type --- a list of detected points --- and scored by the same code.
Figure~\ref{fig:protocol} shows the path.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/Chapter4/ch4_fig5_protocol.png}
\caption[The shared evaluation protocol]{Both arms are reduced to point lists and scored through a
single code path. The only thing that differs between the two routes is the score map itself.}
\label{fig:protocol}
\end{figure}

Peak extraction is the same function call for both arms, with a non-maximum suppression radius of
\ProtocolNms{} cells. Scoring is restricted to the interior region where a full window fits, so no
detector is penalised for map edges it cannot legitimately test.

\subsection{Matching}

A detection matches a truth point if it falls within the tolerance gate,
\begin{equation}
|\Delta r| \le G_r \quad\text{and}\quad |\Delta v| \le G_v ,
\end{equation}
and matching is \textbf{one-to-one and optimal}, computed by Hungarian assignment so that a single
detection cannot claim two targets. A matched detection is a true positive; an unmatched truth is a
false negative; an unmatched detection is a false positive.

One rule prevents double counting: a detection lying inside the gate of a real target but not
selected by the assignment is \emph{ignored} rather than counted as a false alarm. It is a near
miss of a genuine target, not a spurious response.

\subsection{Operating point}

The headline comparison is $\Pd$ at matched \emph{measured} $\Pfa$, swept per condition. This is
stricter than the field's usual practice of matching at design $\Pfa$, and
Section~\ref{sec:alphacal} showed why it is necessary: the achieved rate and the design rate are
not the same number, and the discrepancy differs between variants. Matching at the achieved rate
removes that as a confound.

\subsection{Tolerance}

The pre-registered gate is $\pm\ProtocolGate$ bin. A gate of $\pm\ProtocolGateRobust$ bins is
reported \emph{beside} it, never instead of it. Reporting both separates detection from
localisation: a detector that finds a target but places it two bins away scores differently under
the two gates, and collapsing to a single tolerance hides that distinction.

This choice has a consequence that must not be quietly dropped. The wider gate is where the network
loses on one condition. Reporting only the tolerant gate would hide the single condition where the
classical arm wins, so both are always shown.

\subsection{Statistics}

Differences are assessed by a paired bootstrap over frames, with \ProtocolBootstrap{} resamples;
both arms are evaluated on the same frames, so the pairing is exact by construction. Because seven
conditions are scored, significance is assessed with a Bonferroni correction, giving a per-condition
threshold of $\alpha = \ProtocolBonf$ against a family-wise $0.05$.

\section{Conclusion}

Both detectors are now fully specified, and so is the instrument that will measure them.

The classical arm uses a \CfarWinR{}\,$\times$\,\CfarWinD{} window whose guard count follows the
measured point spread function and whose shape was selected on a split reserved for that purpose.
Its four variants have multipliers calibrated by Monte-Carlo rather than taken from a closed form
that, as measured here, does not deliver the design false-alarm rate on this data.

The learned arm is a \TinyParams{}-parameter fully convolutional network whose receptive field
composes to exactly the CFAR window. The limits of that match are stated in
Section~\ref{sec:matched}: the two arms share a footprint, not a decision rule, and the network is
not forced to discard the guard cells that CFAR must.

The protocol reduces both arms to point lists and scores them through one code path, at matched
measured false-alarm rate, across two tolerances, with paired bootstrap intervals and correction
for multiple comparisons. It was fixed, and the network configuration frozen, before the test split
was read.

Chapter~\ref{chp:synthetic} now reports what that measurement returned.
