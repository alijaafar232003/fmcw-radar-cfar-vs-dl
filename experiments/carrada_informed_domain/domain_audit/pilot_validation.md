# Phase 5 — pilot validation of the CARRADA-informed synthetic domain

700 pilot frames, generated by the frozen project generator with the background intervention
installed, audited by **the same script and the same protocol** as CARRADA train and the original
simulator (`scripts/audit_domain.py`, background definition imported from R0's own
`r0_background.py`). No CARRADA data was read to produce the pilot; the generator consumes only the
already-frozen `train_derived_targets.json`.

## What the intervention does

| component | mechanism | train-derived setting |
|---|---|---|
| multi-look smoothness | incoherent power average of $L$ independent background realisations sharing one deterministic target field | $L \sim \mathrm{Unif}\{7, 8\}$, mean 7.5 (detrended train estimate 7.42, per-sequence 7.08–7.88) |
| range dependence | measured unit-mean-power range profile applied to the background only | 256-bin profile, 9-bin smoothed |
| ridge structure | narrow strong zero-Doppler ridge in essentially every frame | `width_bins=0`, `cnr_db ~ Unif(19.4, 26.4)`, prevalence 0.990 |

`generate_dechirped_cpi`, the Blackman range/Doppler windows and `compute_rd_map` are the frozen
originals. Targets, labels, placement, separations, amplitude ranges, the SNR sweep and the strata
are copied **verbatim** from `configs/bench2_train.yaml` by `make_configs.py`, which asserts that
every key except `dataset` and `save` compares equal to the original.

## The design changed after the data was consulted — and it got simpler

At Stop Point A I proposed a **range-dependent** look count, on the reading that the far block
(210–246) was a second, spikier regime at ≈2.1 looks. That reading was wrong. Dividing every cell
by its own range bin's mean background power brings that block from $L = 2.11$ to $L = 7.51$, in
line with all the others:

| block | raw dB spread → $L$ | detrended dB spread → $L$ |
|---|---|---|
| 10–60 | 1.655 → 7.38 | 1.777 → 6.46 |
| 60–110 | 1.674 → 7.22 | 1.680 → 7.17 |
| 110–160 | 1.611 → 7.75 | 1.649 → 7.42 |
| 160–210 | 1.610 → 7.77 | 1.600 → 7.86 |
| **210–246** | **3.375 → 2.11** | **1.639 → 7.51** |

CARRADA's speckle is uniform across range at ≈7.4 looks, and the whole far-block anomaly — the
apparent regime change *and* its inflated lag-1 correlations in both axes — is the steep mean
roll-off inside it acting as a common-mode component. So **one** look count plus **one** mean
profile reproduces it, and a range-dependent look count would have double-counted the same effect.

## Why, exactly — the component decomposition

Background-only frames, components switched on one at a time
(`results/intervention_decomposition.json`):

| arm | dB spread | implied $L$ | lag-1 range | lag-1 Doppler | ridge peak | half-power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original: 1 look, no profile, no ridge | 5.568 | 1.00 | 0.571 | 0.579 | — | — |
| + multi-look only | 1.589 | 7.96 | **0.566** | **0.582** | — | — |
| + multi-look + ridge | 1.585 | 8.00 | 0.567 | 0.582 | 23.2 | 1.00 |
| + multi-look + range profile | 2.152 | 4.55 | **0.692** | **0.707** | — | — |
| full intervention | 2.150 | 4.56 | 0.692 | 0.706 | 23.2 | 1.00 |
| **CARRADA, frame level** | **2.227** | **4.28** | **0.557** | **0.228** | **23.0** | **1.00** |
| **CARRADA, within block 10–60** | **1.655** | **7.37** | **0.459** | **0.025** | — | — |

Four things follow, and they settle the accounting:

1. **Multi-look is a clean intervention.** It moves the spread from 5.57 dB to 1.59 dB and leaves
   both correlations untouched (0.571 → 0.566, 0.579 → 0.582) — exactly as the theory requires,
   since averaging independent looks divides the variance by $L$ and preserves the correlation
   coefficient.
2. **The range profile is the sole cause of the regression**, in both axes.
3. **It is also what makes the frame-level spread match.** Within-block 1.59 dB against CARRADA's
   1.655; frame-level 2.15 dB against CARRADA's 2.227. One component is responsible for a close
   match and for a regression, so they cannot be separated by tuning it.
4. **The inflation itself is a real property of the target domain.** CARRADA's own within-block
   lag-1 range is 0.459 against 0.557 at frame level, and its within-block lag-1 Doppler is 0.025
   against 0.228. The profile's common-mode effect happens in the real data too.

So what the intervention cannot fix is the **baseline speckle correlation**, which the frozen
Blackman windows set at ≈0.57 in both axes against CARRADA's 0.459 (range) and 0.025 (Doppler).
Adding a correct profile on top of a baseline that is already too high overshoots. Both regressions
and the wide ridge skirt are the same frozen-window constraint that made Doppler correlation a
declared residual in the first place — CARRADA's ridge falls to 3 dB by $\pm1$ bin, which is
sharper than a Blackman mainlobe can be for any input.

**The trade was taken deliberately.** Dropping the range profile would return lag-1 range to
within 0.016 of CARRADA and restore the Doppler error to 0.348, at the cost of abandoning range
non-stationarity — one of the three properties the intervention exists to match, and the one whose
absence coincides with Chapter 7's largest TinyCNN deficit (block 10–60). The profile stays.

## The SNR calibration survived — measured, not assumed

Incoherent averaging preserves mean target and mean background power and divides only the
background variance, and the range profile is unit-mean in power for the same reason. Both were
asserted rather than trusted:

| check | result |
|---|---|
| range profile mean power | 1.000000000000 (exact, asserted) |
| shaped-noise per-sample variance vs requested | −0.17 % |
| flat-profile variance vs `add_awgn_var` | +0.63 % |
| **realised mean background power, new vs original** | **0.9751 (−0.11 dB)** |
| recorded `cell_snr_db`, original | mean 16.24, median 15.30, p5–p95 0.20–35.02 |
| recorded `cell_snr_db`, CARRADA-informed | mean 15.83, median 14.93, p5–p95 0.07–33.84 |

The `cell_snr_db` path is byte-identical code — it measures a noise-free clean map and divides by
`noise_var`, neither of which the intervention touches — so the residual difference is sampling
noise across different frame seeds, not a systematic shift.

One consequence to record: because the background now varies with range by design, the *local*
background a detector sees at far range is lower than at near range, while `cell_snr_db` keeps its
original global-reference definition. That is a property of the target domain being reproduced, not
a change to the target model, but it means `cell_snr_db` is no longer interchangeable with local
SNR in the new domain.

## Residuals, stated for the record

1. **Lag-1 Doppler speckle correlation is not matched** (0.58 against 0.02 within-block) and its
   frame-level figure is made worse by the range profile. Frozen slow-time window; changing it
   would change the target PSF, the measured mainlobe width, and the guard geometry that makes the
   CFAR comparison fair.
2. **Lag-1 range correlation moves from 0.016 to 0.131 of error** at frame level, same cause.
3. **The ridge skirt is wider than CARRADA's** (4.9 vs 1.8 bins above 3 dB) although the core
   matches (half-power 1.31 vs 1.01 bins). Same frozen-window constraint.
4. **Gamma $k$ overshoots** (5.005 vs 3.975) while dB spread matches to 0.023. CARRADA's
   frame-level moment-$k$ is dragged down by the fixed range-localised bright structure the audit
   found near bin 100, which this generator deliberately does not model. The robust statistic is
   the one that matches.
5. **Fixed range-localised bright structure is not modelled**, and neither is extended target
   morphology. Both are named future work.

## Verdict against the Phase 5 criterion

> the intervention should demonstrably reduce the measured domain discrepancy for the properties it
> was designed to target.

**Met, for all three targeted property groups**, by factors of 3× to 150×. Two untargeted
correlation statistics and the ridge skirt regressed, all three traceable to one frozen constraint
that was declared before the generator was written. No parameter was tuned against CARRADA test,
and no summary number was hand-fitted: every setting comes from `train_derived_targets.json` or
from the one measured `cnr_db` → peak-over-floor mapping in `ridge_calibration.json`.
